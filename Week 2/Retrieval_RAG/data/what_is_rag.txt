What is RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)

RAG is a technique that improves LLM responses by injecting relevant external knowledge into the prompt at query time. Instead of relying solely on what the model learned during training, RAG retrieves up-to-date or domain-specific information and gives it to the model as context.

The core problem RAG solves is called "knowledge cutoff" — LLMs are trained on data up to a certain date and cannot know things that happened after that. RAG fixes this by connecting the model to a live or private knowledge source.

RAG has two distinct phases. The indexing phase happens once offline: documents are chunked, embedded into vectors, and stored in a vector database. The retrieval phase happens at query time: the user's question is embedded, similar chunks are fetched from the vector DB, and those chunks are injected into the prompt before the LLM generates an answer.

Why not just put the entire document in the prompt? For small documents, you can. But for large corpora — thousands of internal documents, a 500-page manual, or a codebase — stuffing everything into context is impossible due to token limits, and expensive even when it fits. RAG solves both problems by retrieving only the relevant subset.

The quality of a RAG system depends more on retrieval quality than on the LLM itself. A weak retriever feeding irrelevant chunks to a strong model produces bad answers. This is why techniques like hybrid search, reranking, and query transformation exist — they all target retrieval quality, not generation quality.

Common failure modes in RAG include: chunks that are too large (dilutes relevance), chunks that are too small (loses context), poor overlap between chunks (splits sentences mid-thought), and no metadata filtering (retrieves from wrong documents).
